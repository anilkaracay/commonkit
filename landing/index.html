Social money for shared things

Shared things that pay for their own upkeep.

COMMONKIT gives every shared machine its own keyless NEAR account. It funds its upkeep from usage fees, opens the upkeep task itself, and opens up to the people who keep it alive.

The problem

In a group chat, the person who fixed it disappears.

  1. 01

    Upkeep is invisible

    Someone cleans the nozzle at midnight. A week later nobody remembers who, and nobody thanks them with anything that lasts.

  2. 02

    The money sits with one admin

    Fees and spare parts run through one person's pocket and spreadsheet. When they leave, the system leaves with them.

  3. 03

    Rules nobody can enforce

    "Only trained people on the laser" holds as long as someone happens to be in the room.

The idea

Give the machine its own account.

Not a wallet someone holds. A keyless NEAR account that runs the same contract on every machine. People reserve it, the bridge next to it only unlocks for a valid reservation, the machine asks for upkeep when its own counters say so, and the person who does the work earns access to it.

  • Money: fees in, capped float, the rest to the DAO
  • Access: reservations and earned rights

Where the money goes

The machine keeps a small float. The rest goes home.

Why onchain

Access nobody can quietly take back.

A bounded agent

The agent can ask. The contract decides.

Each asset has an operations agent with a function-call key. It can open an upkeep task only when the machine's own on-chain counters crossed the rule's threshold, within the reward caps and the period quota. It can never approve work, move money or mint rights.

Why NEAR

A machine needs a name, a narrow key and a treasury it can't empty.
