Shared things that pay for their own upkeep. And open up to the people who keep them alive.

Today a shared machine's money and rules live in one admin's spreadsheet. On COMMONKIT they live in the machine's own keyless account on NEAR: its fees fund its upkeep, it opens its own maintenance tasks, and everything above a small float goes to the community's treasury. No one can drain it, and no one can take back what you earned.

Part I

The books of one machine.

Four figures explain where a member's money goes. Each one was written by a transaction on NEAR testnet, step by step, in the order below.

0.5NEAR fee, step 2

i.The fee is revenue.

Ali books two 30-minute slots at 0.25 NEAR each. The fee lands in the printer's float, the small amount it keeps for its own upkeep.

2NEAR deposit, step 2

ii.The deposit is not.

The refundable deposit is held apart as a liability. It is never used for fees, rewards, sweeps or proposal bonds, and it went back to Ali after a clean return.

3NEAR float cap

iii.The machine keeps only a little.

The float has a cap, set in the printer's profile. At step 6 a second fee pushed it to 3.0499 NEAR, so the contract swept the difference out in the same callback.

0.0499NEAR swept, step 6

iv.The rest belongs to the community.

The makerlab Sputnik DAO treasury went from 5.6511 to 5.7010 NEAR. Big payments, like a nozzle kit at step 10, leave the treasury only through a DAO proposal that the printer itself opens.

What a machine's account may holddeposits + reserved rewards + float (≤ cap) + storage reserve

In the app

Every machine keeps an account card.
